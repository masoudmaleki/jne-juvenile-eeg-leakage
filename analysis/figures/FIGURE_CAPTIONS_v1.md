**Figure 1. Data leakage inflates classification of offenders versus controls.**
(a) Subject-level ROC-AUC of SVM and random-forest pipelines on the published Excel features. N1–N2 use row-level (epoch-level) cross-validation; N3–N4 and the nested pipeline (P) use subject-level cross-validation. N1 and N3 select features on all data, N2 and N4 select them within the cross-validation loop. Whiskers show the 2.5–97.5% range across CV repetitions. (b) When labels are shuffled at the subject level, the naive pipeline (N1) still reaches an AUC of 0.79–0.85, whereas the nested pipeline falls to chance. (c) Golden Distance gives the same ranking as AUC.

**Figure 2. The exploratory separation is graded and does not transfer to the second offender subgroup.**
(a) Subject-level ROC-AUC (sg vs cg) of a nested SVM on channel-level band power, with grey bars showing the 95% range of the shuffled-label null. Separation drops from eyes-open to eyes-closed epochs and drops further when frontal channels are removed; the model trained on sg and cg does not separate sg2 from controls (transfer AUC 0.57, p = 0.26). The shaded band marks the 0.60–0.65 range for which the pre-specified decision rule defined no outcome. (b) In eyes-closed epochs, relative frontal delta is higher only in sg, whereas relative posterior alpha is lowest in sg2 (median and IQR).

**Figure 3. The single pre-specified hypothesis test, alpha reactivity, shows no group difference.**
(a) Alpha reactivity index, (C − O)/(C + O), from posterior channels in the four 50-s eyes-closed and eyes-open epochs, shown by subgroup. Offenders (sg + sg2, n = 70) did not differ from controls (n = 66; Mann–Whitney p = 0.395). (b) Rank-biserial correlation and (c) Hodges–Lehmann shift, with bootstrap 95% CIs. Panel (c) also shows the age- and EMG-adjusted OLS estimate; all intervals include zero.

**Figure 4. Pre-specified spectral features do not separate offenders from controls, and large effects can be excluded.**
ROC-AUC of an L2-regularised logistic regression on 14 pre-specified relative and aperiodic features from the long eyes-closed block. For offenders vs controls the subject-bootstrap 95% CI is 0.44–0.63, which excludes AUC > 0.63 (Cohen's d ≈ 0.48). Open markers show variability across CV repetitions and are not confidence intervals.

**Figure 5. Cohort composition, provenance of the derived features, and recording-period confound.**
(a) Participant flow from the 140 published recordings to the samples used in each analysis. (b) The derived Excel features cover 112 participants; 12 of them match no published recording, and the 25 sg2 participants and 15 other published participants are absent. (c) Recording dates by group show that the sg and control recordings do not overlap in time.
